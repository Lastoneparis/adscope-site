Where your app actually ranks, measured one storefront at a time

AdScope polls the App Store for the keywords you care about, stores every sample it takes, and shows you the movement. It is a tracker for one developer’s own apps, not an agency dashboard.

iPhone, iPad and Mac are tracked as three separate indexes, because they are three separate indexes. A term that is brutal on iPhone is often half as contested on the Mac App Store.

What it does

  • Rank history that is kept. Every poll is stored as a sample and retained for a year, so a drop can be dated rather than guessed at.
  • Movement, not snapshots. The keyword table sorts by how far a term moved, which is the only column worth reading first.
  • Apple Search Ads, read-only. Connect an API key and AdScope pulls campaigns, daily spend and a 30-day series. It never creates or edits a campaign.
  • Competitor keyword gaps. Terms a competitor ranks for and you do not — scored on measured volume, so a gap with no traffic behind it scores zero.
  • CSV export. Rank history leaves the app in a format a spreadsheet can read.

The limits, plainly

Apple does not publish search volume. AdScope infers it from autocomplete, which counts suggestions rather than relevance — a term can show volume because it collides with an unrelated popular query. Treat the number as a ranking signal, not a traffic forecast.

The store rate-limits. Requests are throttled to roughly one per second and cached for fifteen minutes. A large keyword set takes minutes to refresh, and refreshing it twice in a row will not make it faster.

Search Ads needs your own credentials. There is no AdScope account and no server in the middle; the key lives in your Keychain on your device.
